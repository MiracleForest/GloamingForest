## Question

Bedrock's registries that upstream extends are closed enums or hard-coded lists that vanilla clients also
hold: `Enchant::Type` (42 enchantments), `MobEffectIds` (38 effects), `BannerPattern::mPatterns()`,
the trim material registry, the record component's sound event, the creative catalog's groups. For
each: can upstream's entry exist (server and client mod), and does a vanilla client that receives it keep
working and show something sensible?

## Why it matters

- Upstream's four enchantments (fire_react, chill_aura, destruction, renewal; U10) and the frosted effect
  (U10, used by the ice bow, ice sword, chill aura, snow queen, ...) are referenced across W1.
- Nine boss banner patterns (U5), six trim materials (U7), nine music discs (U10) and four creative tabs
  (F9 / F1) are upstream content that vanilla clients will meet on a BDS.
- Item tooltips, glint, rarity colours, bars and the effect HUD are part of "every screen matches".

## What was tried

1. **Enchantments**: the levels live in the stack's user data (`gf:enchant` {id: level}), are written as
   gray lore lines named like enchantments ("Fire React II", Java's `Enchantment#getFullname`), and an
   empty vanilla enchantment list (`ench: []`) marks the stack enchanted for the glint
   (`src/feature/enchant/Enchant.cpp`, `updateGlint`). The engine paths are hooked where Bedrock
   computes them: `EnchantUtils::combineEnchantedItems` (anvil: Java's AnvilMenu with one mutable set,
   exclusive sets against everything joined so far, vanilla enchantments the engine merged checked
   against the custom ones, supported items unless the player builds instantly, anvil cost), the
   player of the running item stack request from `ItemStackNetManagerServer::_processQueue`,
   `EnchantUtils::removeEnchants` and `CraftHandlerGrindstone::_getExperienceFromItem` (grindstone),
   `EnchantingContainerManagerModel::recalculateOptions` + `CraftHandlerEnchant::$_handleCraftAction` +
   `ItemStackBase::saveEnchantsToUserData` (enchanting table). All MCAPI. A real anvil is driven in the
   client e2e (sword + book, both at I). Vanilla clients preview the anvil themselves, so for an
   enchanted book whose enchantments are all custom the server offers a form instead
   (`src/feature/enchant/AnvilFallback.cpp`: a use of the anvil with such a book in hand, through
   the interaction dispatch; AnvilMenu#createResult / #onTake numbers). The flags treasure /
   tradeable / discoverable come from the enchantment tags for registered specs too
   (`ext/item/Enchant.h` `setFlags` overrides).
2. **Effects**: a custom effect is native state with a vanilla carrier (`src/feature/effects/Effects.cpp`):
   frosted = Slowness of the same amplifier with hidden particles, persisted as an actor tag
   (`gf_fx|<id>|<amplifier>|<ticks>|<hidden>|<displaced>`), Java's `MobEffectInstance#update` and
   `#tick` (hidden weaker / longer applications that resume). The carrier is the custom effect's own
   instance only while it is: a vanilla instance of the same effect that is as strong and lasts as
   long is left alone; a weaker one is replaced and put back afterwards with its remaining time; a
   carrier lost on its own (a single `/effect` removal, an expired vanilla instance that carried it) is
   put back; milk and `/effect clear` (`Actor::removeAllEffects`) end the custom effect too. A state
   read back from its tag (rewritten every second, on every change and at world save) follows the
   carrier the actor kept, so an unload between two writes does not make the carrier look foreign
   (`resync`). Companions
   get `effects.sync`; the client mod hides the carrier from Bedrock's own effect HUD
   (`HudMobEffectsRenderer::$render`) and draws the custom effect's icon where Java's
   `Gui#renderEffects` puts it, and the effect list next to the inventory as
   `EffectRenderingInventoryScreen` (`ll::event::AfterUIRenderEvent`; GUI textures as UI meshes, spike S5).
3. **Banner patterns**: read `BannerPattern::mPatterns()`; a banner whose `Patterns` list holds an id no
   client knows (`gf_test_pattern`) is saved / loaded on the server and given to a vanilla client; in the
   client build the pattern is appended to the registry (`BannerPattern(name, nameID, hidden)`, MCAPI)
   and the same banner is photographed in a local world (`item.spike_banner_append`).
4. **Trim materials**: read `TrimMaterialRegistryHardCodedInit::getMaterials()`. `ext::addTrimMaterial`
   (`src/feature/item/Trims.cpp`) appends registered materials right after
   `TrimMaterialRegistry::initializeServer` (hook) or at world ready (BDS), before players join; the
   spike's runs register `iron_darker` at load (an id outside the vanilla list whose palette texture
   the vanilla pack has): the scenarios' prelaunch step (`tools/e2e/{server,client}/scenarios/
   item.prelaunch.ps1`) leaves `<mod>/s6-trim-probe.flag` next to the development build, so no other
   world (the `full` run included) carries the fixture material. `item.spike_client` appends
   `gold_darker` (palette present) and `gf_test_trim` (no palette) while the player is in the world
   (and `iron_darker` too where no flag registered it at load: the BDS host of the client's remote
   modes) and re-sends `TrimDataPacket` with the materials and no patterns (a client adds the
   packet's patterns to the ones it has, `TrimPatternRegistry::initializeFromTrimPatterns`, and logs
   "Overwriting pattern template. Duplicate entries with template item: ..." for every vanilla
   pattern it already took from the join's packet; the client mod's `Trims.cpp` also lets only the
   missing patterns through that function, for servers that re-send them); the player gets diamond chestplates trimmed with each, and one with emerald (hotbar 6..9);
   `item.spike_client_clear` takes the appended ones out again.
5. **Record sounds**: read the `RecordItemComponent` (`ComponentItem::getComponent`,
   `RecordItemComponent::getIdentifier()`) of eight vanilla discs and the alternative of their
   `SoundEventIdentifier` (`std::variant<monostate, LevelSoundEvent, std::string>`)
   (`item.spike_record_sounds`). The spike's runs add a probe BP (prelaunch step,
   `tools/e2e/server/scenarios/item.s6probe.ps1`) with a data-driven disc `gf_s6:disc`; its first
   version named a custom `"sound_event": "gf_s6.disc"`, the current one leaves it out and plays
   through `gf::item::ext::setRecordSound` (`src/feature/item/Records.cpp`: hooks on
   `JukeboxBlockActor::_startRecordSound`, `stopPlayingRecord` and `setRecord`).
6. **Catalog**: read the server's `CreativeItemRegistry` (groups, entries) through
   `level.getItemRegistry().getCreativeItemRegistry()`; write data into its entries (the creative
   default enchantments of `showEnchantmentsOnItems`, units' default data) at world ready. Whether
   entries can be added at runtime: `CreativeItemRegistry::newItemEntry`'s declaration.
7. **Attachables**: armour models on the body are resource pack attachables (F2's
   `tools/models/attachable.ps1` over F1's BP items, in the mod's RP that BDS also sends vanilla
   clients). `item.spike_attachables` puts upstream's fiery helmet (F2's own geometry
   `geometry.gloamingforest.fiery_armor.helmet`) and naga chestplate (the humanoid armour geometry with
   Upstream's naga scale texture) into every real player's armour slots (`Mob::setArmor` + `sendArmor`);
   the client e2e photographs the player through `/camera @s set minecraft:third_person_front`
   (`attachables-front.png`); `item.spike_attachables_clear` puts back what was there.

## Conclusion

- **Enchantments: yes.** User data + lore + the empty vanilla list give vanilla clients Java's look
  (gray enchantment lines, aqua name, glint). The anvil, grindstone and table paths are hooked on the
  server and in the client mod. Upstream 4.8 puts none of its enchantments into
  `#minecraft:in_enchanting_table` (or `tradeable` / `on_random_loot`), so the table never offers them,
  as on Java (also when an owner re-registers them: the flags are the tags'); they come from loot
  (F10's loot functions call `gf::enchant::set`). Vanilla clients apply upstream-only books through the
  anvil form (use the anvil holding the book, close its screen), with Java's result and cost.
- **Effects: yes**, through carriers that never cost the player a vanilla effect. Vanilla clients see the
  carrier's icon and predict its movement effect (Slowness for frosted = upstream's attribute modifier); the
  client mod shows upstream's icon where Java does, hides the carrier, and lists the effect next to the
  inventory.
- **Banner patterns: extensible, not shown to vanilla clients.** The registry is a plain vector with
  MCAPI constructors: the client mod appends upstream's nine patterns at mod load (before the game hands out
  pattern pointers; a later append is only safe with spare capacity) with their textures in the RP.
  A vanilla client survives unknown pattern ids but draws them as a full `base` field with an
  untranslated line, so U5 strips (or maps to the nearest vanilla pattern) upstream's ids in banner data sent
  to non-companions.
- **Trim materials: the registry, the tooltip and the smithing table yes; the inventory icon no.** The
  level's `TrimMaterialRegistry` is a vector the server sends in `TrimDataPacket`, at join and again
  after a change; `ext::addTrimMaterial` puts a unit's materials into every level's registry as it
  initialises. Clients then list the trim in the tooltip in the material's colour, but draw armour
  trimmed with a material outside Bedrock's own list as an empty slot: the whole item icon is gone,
  not only the trim (registered before or after the join, with or without a palette texture, client
  mod or vanilla client). U7: register upstream's six with `ext::addTrimMaterial` and ship the palettes;
  the client mod must draw these items itself (the icon generator, `ArmorTrimIconGenerator`, has no
  exported API in 26.51.7), and vanilla clients need upstream's trims stripped (or mapped to a vanilla
  material) in the item data sent to them, or the item is invisible in their inventory.
- **Record sounds: through the jukebox hook, not the BP component.** The record component's sound is a
  variant with a sound-name alternative, but the BP parser binds `sound_event` to the LevelSoundEvent
  enum and refuses upstream's names. Upstream's discs (U10) are BP items with `minecraft:record` (duration,
  comparator signal) and no `sound_event`; U10 registers each disc's JukeboxSong event with
  `gf::item::ext::setRecordSound`, and the jukebox plays it by name (`PlaySoundPacket` through
  `gf::fx`, heard by vanilla clients and the client mod alike) and stops it with the record.
- **Creative tabs: yes, as catalog groups.** The BP `item_catalog` declares the four groups (F1's import
  of TFCreativeTabs), the server's registry holds them, `gf::catalog` reads them back in order; item
  data of catalog entries (the default enchantments, units' defaults through `ext/item/Catalog.h`) is
  written into the registry. BDS cannot add entries at runtime (`newItemEntry` is client-only), so
  variants of one item (upstream's three caskets, the glass sword's lore version) need their own catalog
  entries in the data.
- **Attachables: yes.** F2's attachables of upstream's armour draw on the player's body from the mod's RP (`attachables-front.png`: the orange fiery helmet with its own geometry, the green naga chestplate); BDS sends the same RP to vanilla clients. The armour items need U7's `minecraft:wearable` for players to equip them themselves.
- Tooltips, rarity, bars: tooltips are server lore in the holder's language for every client; the
  GLOAMING rarity's dark green name (with `ItemStack#getRarity`'s enchanted bump) and `setBar` bars are
  drawn by the client mod (hooks on `ItemStackBase::_getHoverFormattingPrefix`,
  `InventoryItemRenderer::$render` + `MinecraftUIRenderContext::$endSharedMeshBatch` at High priority so
  bars land on top of F12's GUI item renderers); the bar is computed from the slot's real stack (the
  slot's container model by the renderer's `collection_name` / `collection_index`), so it follows item
  data such as BrittleFlaskItem's doses. Vanilla clients see Bedrock's own name colour and durability bar.

## Fallback and follow-ups

- If the empty `ench` list stops giving the glint after an update, a placeholder vanilla enchantment is
  no way out (the client would list it); the fallback is the `minecraft:glint` item component on items
  that are always enchanted, and lore without glint otherwise.
- U5: append upstream's banner patterns in the client mod at load; strip them for vanilla clients.
- U7: register the trim materials with `ext::addTrimMaterial`; ship the palettes; draw armour trimmed
  with upstream's materials in the client mod (the game draws the whole item as an empty slot) and strip
  Upstream trims for vanilla clients (or the item is invisible in their inventory).
- U10: the discs' `minecraft:record` without `sound_event`, their songs through `ext::setRecordSound`; the frosted effect registers with
  `.carrier = "minecraft:slowness"`, upstream's colour 0x56CBFD and harmful.
- F1: emit `minecraft:fuel` / `minecraft:compostable` components for upstream's furnace fuels / compostables so
  vanilla clients accept them in the fuel slot (the server and the client mod apply the data maps
  already).
- Vanilla clients: the anvil screen previews the vanilla part only; upstream-only books go through the anvil
  form (sent when the anvil screen closes), books with vanilla enchantments too through the screen (the server adds the
  custom ones).
