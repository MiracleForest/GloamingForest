# S6: Can TF's enchantments, effects, banner patterns, trim materials, music discs and creative tabs exist on Bedrock without breaking vanilla clients?

| | |
|---|---|
| Unit | F9 |
| Status | answered with a fallback (enchantments, effects, catalog: yes; banner patterns: extensible in the client mod, stripped for vanilla clients; trim materials: extensible before players join; record sounds: by name, to be closed by U10's first BP disc) |
| Date | 2026-11-02 |
| Game / LeviLamina | 1.26.51 / 26.51.7 |

## Question

Bedrock's registries that TF extends are closed enums or hard-coded lists that vanilla clients also
hold: `Enchant::Type` (42 enchantments), `MobEffectIds` (38 effects), `BannerPattern::mPatterns()`,
the trim material registry, the record component's sound event, the creative catalog's groups. For
each: can TF's entry exist (server and client mod), and does a vanilla client that receives it keep
working and show something sensible?

## Why it matters

- TF's four enchantments (fire_react, chill_aura, destruction, renewal; U10) and the frosted effect
  (U10, used by the ice bow, ice sword, chill aura, snow queen, ...) are referenced across W1.
- Nine boss banner patterns (U5), six trim materials (U7), nine music discs (U10) and four creative tabs
  (F9 / F1) are TF content that vanilla clients will meet on a BDS.
- Item tooltips, glint, rarity colours, bars and the effect HUD are part of "every screen matches".

## What was tried

1. **Enchantments**: the levels live in the stack's user data (`gf:enchant` {id: level}), are written as
   gray lore lines named like enchantments ("Fire React II", Java's `Enchantment#getFullname`), and an
   empty vanilla enchantment list (`ench: []`) marks the stack enchanted for the glint
   (`src/feature/enchant/Enchant.cpp`, `updateGlint`). The engine paths are hooked where Bedrock
   computes them: `EnchantUtils::combineEnchantedItems` (anvil: Java's AnvilMenu with one mutable set,
   exclusive sets against everything joined so far, vanilla enchantments the engine merged checked
   against the custom ones, supported items unless the player builds instantly, anvil cost), the
   player of the running item stack request from `ItemStackNetManagerServer::_processQueue`,
   `EnchantUtils::removeEnchants` and `CraftHandlerGrindstone::_getExperienceFromItem` (grindstone),
   `EnchantingContainerManagerModel::recalculateOptions` + `CraftHandlerEnchant::$_handleCraftAction` +
   `ItemStackBase::saveEnchantsToUserData` (enchanting table). All MCAPI. A real anvil is driven in the
   client e2e (sword + book, both at I).
2. **Effects**: a custom effect is native state with a vanilla carrier (`src/feature/effects/Effects.cpp`):
   frosted = Slowness of the same amplifier with hidden particles, persisted as an actor tag
   (`gf_fx|<id>|<amplifier>|<ticks>|<hidden>|<displaced>`), Java's `MobEffectInstance#update` and
   `#tick` (hidden weaker / longer applications that resume). The carrier is the custom effect's own
   instance only while it is: a vanilla instance of the same effect that is as strong and lasts as
   long is left alone; a weaker one is replaced and put back afterwards with its remaining time; a
   carrier lost on its own (a single `/effect` removal, an expired vanilla instance that carried it) is
   put back; milk and `/effect clear` (`Actor::removeAllEffects`) end the custom effect too. Companions
   get `effects.sync`; the client mod hides the carrier from Bedrock's own effect HUD
   (`HudMobEffectsRenderer::$render`) and draws the custom effect's icon where Java's
   `Gui#renderEffects` puts it, and the effect list next to the inventory as
   `EffectRenderingInventoryScreen` (`ll::event::AfterUIRenderEvent`; GUI textures as UI meshes, spike S5).
3. **Banner patterns**: read `BannerPattern::mPatterns()`; a banner whose `Patterns` list holds an id no
   client knows (`gf_test_pattern`) is saved / loaded on the server and given to a vanilla client; in the
   client build the pattern is appended to the registry (`BannerPattern(name, nameID, hidden)`, MCAPI)
   and the same banner is photographed in a local world (`item.spike_banner_append`).
4. **Trim materials**: read `TrimMaterialRegistryHardCodedInit::getMaterials()`; append a material
   (`gf_test_trim`, a copy of emerald's with `§2` and the nether star as its item) to the level's
   `TrimMaterialRegistry` (`Level::getTrimMaterialRegistry()`), send every real player a new
   `TrimDataPacket` (patterns from `TrimPatternRegistry::getAllEntries()`), and give a diamond
   chestplate trimmed with it next to an emerald-trimmed one (`item.spike_trim_materials`,
   `item.spike_client`).
5. **Record sounds**: read the `RecordItemComponent` (`ComponentItem::getComponent`,
   `RecordItemComponent::getIdentifier()`) of eight vanilla discs and the alternative of their
   `SoundEventIdentifier` (`std::variant<monostate, LevelSoundEvent, std::string>`)
   (`item.spike_record_sounds`).
6. **Catalog**: read the server's `CreativeItemRegistry` (groups, entries) through
   `level.getItemRegistry().getCreativeItemRegistry()`; write data into its entries (the creative
   default enchantments of `showEnchantmentsOnItems`, units' default data) at world ready. Whether
   entries can be added at runtime: `CreativeItemRegistry::newItemEntry`'s declaration.
7. **Attachables**: armour models on the body are resource pack attachables (F2's
   `tools/models/attachable.ps1`, U7's armour); F9 owns no pack paths (owners.tsv) and no armour item
   exists before F1 / U7, so nothing could be exercised here.

## Conclusion

- **Enchantments: yes.** User data + lore + the empty vanilla list give vanilla clients Java's look
  (gray enchantment lines, aqua name, glint). The anvil, grindstone and table paths are hooked on the
  server and in the client mod. TF 4.8 puts none of its enchantments into
  `#minecraft:in_enchanting_table` (or `tradeable` / `on_random_loot`), so the table never offers them,
  as on Java; they come from loot (F10's loot functions call `gf::enchant::set`).
- **Effects: yes**, through carriers that never cost the player a vanilla effect. Vanilla clients see the
  carrier's icon and predict its movement effect (Slowness for frosted = TF's attribute modifier); the
  client mod shows TF's icon where Java does, hides the carrier, and lists the effect next to the
  inventory.
- **Banner patterns: extensible, not shown to vanilla clients.** The registry is a plain vector with
  MCAPI constructors: the client mod appends TF's nine patterns at mod load (before the game hands out
  pattern pointers; a later append is only safe with spare capacity) with their textures in the RP.
  A vanilla client survives unknown pattern ids but draws them as a full `base` field with an
  untranslated line, so U5 strips (or maps to the nearest vanilla pattern) TF's ids in banner data sent
  to non-companions.
- **Trim materials: extensible on the server, at world start only.** The level's `TrimMaterialRegistry`
  is a vector the server sends in `TrimDataPacket`, but a client takes the materials only when it joins:
  an item trimmed with a material appended later is not shown at all (client mod and vanilla). U7 appends
  TF's six materials right after `TrimMaterialRegistry::initializeServer` (before any player joins) and
  ships the palette textures in the RP; fallback: strip TF materials from item data sent to clients that
  joined before (or cannot draw them).
- **Record sounds: by name.** The record component's sound is a variant whose third alternative is a sound
  event name; TF's discs (U10) are BP items with `minecraft:record` naming `twilightforest:music_disc.*`
  events (F3's sound definitions). F9 owns no BP items (owners.tsv), so the first real disc is U10's
  proof; fallback: a jukebox hook plays the disc with `PlaySoundPacket` and keeps the comparator output.
- **Creative tabs: yes, as catalog groups.** The BP `item_catalog` declares the four groups (F1's import
  of TFCreativeTabs), the server's registry holds them, `gf::catalog` reads them back in order; item
  data of catalog entries (the default enchantments, units' defaults through `ext/item/Catalog.h`) is
  written into the registry. BDS cannot add entries at runtime (`newItemEntry` is client-only), so
  variants of one item (TF's three caskets, the glass sword's lore version) need their own catalog
  entries in the data.
- **Attachables**: resource pack attachables of TF's armour (F2's tool, U7's items); not exercisable by F9.
- Tooltips, rarity, bars: tooltips are server lore in the holder's language for every client; the
  TWILIGHT rarity's dark green name (with `ItemStack#getRarity`'s enchanted bump) and `setBar` bars are
  drawn by the client mod (hooks on `ItemStackBase::_getHoverFormattingPrefix`,
  `InventoryItemRenderer::$render` + `MinecraftUIRenderContext::$endSharedMeshBatch` at High priority so
  bars land on top of F12's GUI item renderers); the bar is computed from the slot's real stack (the
  slot's container model by the renderer's `collection_name` / `collection_index`), so it follows item
  data such as BrittleFlaskItem's doses. Vanilla clients see Bedrock's own name colour and durability bar.

## Fallback and follow-ups

- If the empty `ench` list stops giving the glint after an update, a placeholder vanilla enchantment is
  no way out (the client would list it); the fallback is the `minecraft:glint` item component on items
  that are always enchanted, and lore without glint otherwise.
- U5: append TF's banner patterns in the client mod at load; strip them for vanilla clients.
- U7: append the trim materials to the level's registry before players join (after
  `initializeServer`); ship the palettes.
- U10: the discs' `minecraft:record` with a custom `sound_event`; the frosted effect registers with
  `.carrier = "minecraft:slowness"`, TF's colour 0x56CBFD and harmful.
- F1: emit `minecraft:fuel` / `minecraft:compostable` components for TF's furnace fuels / compostables so
  vanilla clients accept them in the fuel slot (the server and the client mod apply the data maps
  already).
- Deviation recorded: the anvil preview of a vanilla client shows the vanilla part only.
