# S1: Can the Twilight Forest be a real dimension on Bedrock, for the client mod and for vanilla clients?

| | |
|---|---|
| Unit | F4 |
| Status | answered |
| Date | 2026-11-02 |
| Game / LeviLamina | 1.26.51 / 26.51.7 |

## Question

Can GloamingForest register `twilightforest:twilight_forest` as a dimension of its own with TF's
`twilight_forest_type` values (min_y -32, height 288, fixed time 13000, coordinate scale 0.125, beds
work), move players (SimulatedPlayers, client-mod players and vanilla Bedrock clients joining a BDS)
into it and back, and have every client render its chunks from y -32 to y 255?

## Why it matters

Every landmark, biome, mob and boss of TF lives in this dimension; every W1 selftest that needs it
calls `ctx.inTwilight()`, and the vanilla-client matrix needs vanilla clients to enter it. The
plan allowed two routes: the game's own dimension definition, or the vendored MoreDimensions route
(fake dimension ids remapped for clients).

## What was tried

1. **Native custom dimension (chosen).** Bedrock 1.26.50 ships data-driven custom dimensions:
   `SharedTypes::v1_26_50::DimensionDefinition` (`minecraft:dimension` with the components
   `minecraft:generation`, `minecraft:dimension_height`, `minecraft:default_biome`),
   `DimensionDefinitionGroup` (read from the behavior packs), `CustomDimension`,
   `DimensionManager::_registerCustomDimensionWithFactory` and the `DimensionDataPacket` that sends
   the definitions (min y, height range, generator, id) to every client. F4 ships
   `assets/pack/bp/dimensions/dimension/twilight_forest.json` (format 1.26.50, void generator,
   `min_y` -32, `height_range` 288, default biome `minecraft:forest`); the engine reads it from a
   sub-folder of `dimensions/`, registers it at world start and gives it an id from the world's
   dimension name table (1000, the first custom id; it is persisted with the world and stays the
   same across restarts). Code: `src/feature/dimension/Dimension.cpp` (diagnostic hooks on
   `ScriptCustomDimensionRegistry::_onReadyForCustomDimensionRegistration` and
   `DimensionManager::_registerCustomDimensionWithFactory`, both MCAPI), `World.cpp`
   (`DimensionManager::tryGetDimensionType`, `Level::getOrCreateDimension`).
2. **TF's dimension_type values the definition can't hold** are added in code:
   - `fixed_time` 13000: a hook on `Dimension::$getTimeOfDay` (virtual, MCAPI) answers the time of
     day of 13000 for the Twilight Forest whatever the level time (server lighting / spawning and
     the client mod's sky).
   - Vanilla clients render the sky from their own clock, so they get a per-player clock
     (`Clock.cpp`). In 1.26 the client clock follows three packets, all of which are corrected for a
     player in the Twilight Forest:
     - `SetTimePacket` (level time),
     - `SyncWorldClocksPacket` (new in 1.26: the world clock registry, `WorldClockRegistryServer`;
       the clock named `VanillaWorldClocks::OVERWORLD_CLOCK_NAME()` is the one the sky follows; it
       is sent at join as `InitializeRegistryData` and when synced as `SyncStateData`),
     - `GameRulesChangedPacket` (doDaylightCycle).
     Packets for one player (`ServerPlayer::$sendNetworkPacket`) are corrected in place before they
     are sent and restored afterwards. Packets the `LoopbackPacketSender` sends to several players
     are followed by a corrected one in the same tick. Entering / leaving players get the Twilight
     / world clock, and the Twilight clock is sent again every 5 s. What really leaves for each
     connection (`NetworkSystem::_sendInternal`) is recorded, and the selftest
     `dimension.clock_watch` checks for 20 s that every tick of a remote player in the dimension
     ends with the time of day 13000.
   - `has_raids` false: a hook on `Village::triggerRaid` (MCAPI) refuses raids of villages in the
     dimension.
   - `coordinate_scale` 0.125, `bed_works` (the engine already allows beds in the custom
     dimension: `mayRespawnViaBed()` is true), `respawn_anchor_works` false and `piglin_safe`
     false (on Bedrock anchors only work and piglins only stay unconverted in the Nether),
     `infiniburn` #infiniburn_overworld (netherrack and magma burn forever in every Bedrock
     dimension), `natural` true (`isNaturalDimension()` is true), `ultrawarm` / `has_ceiling` false
     and `has_skylight` true (the definition's defaults).
   - TF's functions `twilightforest:tp_twilight` / `tp_overworld` work as behavior pack functions:
     `/execute in twilightforest:twilight_forest` accepts the custom dimension's identifier
     (selftest `dimension.execute_in`).
3. **Areas without players.** Java's teleporter loads the destination synchronously and holds it
   with a PORTAL ticket. Bedrock loads chunks around players and ticking areas only; a transient
   ticking area (`TickingAreasManager::addTransientScopedArea`, MCAPI; not saved with the world,
   removed with `removeScopedAreas`) loads the destination while the traveller waits at home
   (`AreaLoader.cpp`, selftest `dimension.area_loader`: an area 77000 blocks away loads without a
   player). Items, mobs and vehicles travel too: the engine's own cross-dimension move of a
   non-player actor lost the item (`Actor::teleport` to another dimension: the entity was neither
   found nor marked removed afterwards), so they move as Java moves them, as a copy made in the
   destination (`Actor::save` + `BlockSource::spawnActor`) with the original removed.
4. **MoreDimensions route: not needed.** It remaps ids for clients that don't know a custom
   dimension; 1.26.50+ clients know them natively, so the remapping would only add risk.

## Evidence

- Server e2e `tools/e2e/server/run.ps1 -Scenario dimension` (exit 0): log lines
  `dimension: twilightforest:twilight_forest registered with the dimension factory as 1000` and
  `dimension: twilightforest:twilight_forest registered as dimension 1000 (name
  'twilightforest:twilight_forest', y -32..256, natural true, bed respawn true, sky true)`; the id is
  the same after `Restart-Server`.
- Selftests `dimension.registered` (id, identifier, height -32..256, scale, fixed time: the time of
  day of the dimension equals the overworld's at 13000 for level times 0..168100, beds) and
  `dimension.enter` (a SimulatedPlayer enters; stone placed and read back at y -32, 0, 64, 255;
  the block source reports min height -32 and max height 256).
- `dimension.portal_travel`: a SimulatedPlayer goes overworld -> Twilight Forest -> overworld
  through portals; it waits at home until the destination loaded, then moves straight to its
  arrival spot. `dimension.item_travel`: sticks dropped into a lit portal are in the Twilight
  Forest 4 ticks later, next to the return portal the teleporter built for them, with no player
  there (`minecraft:item travelled from minecraft:overworld to twilightforest:twilight_forest`).
  `dimension.area_loader`: an area 77000 blocks from any player loads through a transient ticking
  area and is released with its handle. `dimension.execute_in`: `/execute in
  twilightforest:twilight_forest` and the functions `twilightforest/dimension/tp_twilight` /
  `tp_overworld` move a player. Server scenario `dimension`: `SELFTEST DONE pass=19 fail=0`;
  `full`: `pass=37 fail=0`.
- Client e2e `tools/e2e/client/scenarios/dimension.ps1` in every mode (exit 0). The real player
  throws a diamond into a pool (`dimension: portal lit at 101 -61 103 ... by Futrime`), stands in
  the portal (camera fade), travels (`travelled from minecraft:overworld to
  twilightforest:twilight_forest at 808.5 -31.0 826.5`, 0.2 s after entering: the destination was
  loaded while the player waited), and the screenshots show:
  - `twilight-arrival`: the return portal with its grass rim and generated flowers under the
    time-13000 sky (the overworld is at noon);
  - `twilight-up` / `twilight-down`: a stone pillar from y -32 to y 255 with glowstone at both ends;
  - `back-home`: the player back next to the first portal.
- The fixed time on a vanilla client (`-Mode remote-vanilla`). Review round 1 saw a noon sky between
  two dusk ones. The host now logs which packets it corrected, and they were 1.26's world clock
  packets: `corrected a SyncWorldClocks packet for Futrime in the Twilight Forest (via
  sendToClients)` and `(via sendBroadcast)`. With them corrected:
  - the host's `dimension.clock_watch` saw 15 time packets in 20 s, every tick ending with the time
    of day 13000 (`wrong ticks 0`);
  - the sky straight up, sampled 6 times over 20 s, read luminance 84.4, 84.4, 84.4, 84.3, 84.3,
    84.3, against 194.8 for the overworld's noon sky.
  `-Mode remote-mod`: 18 packets, 0 wrong ticks, sky 78.8..84.2 against 185.1.
- Earlier fixes: the client drops camera instructions while it loads a dimension, so the fade times
  itself (in, 1 s hold, 1.5 s out). The client mod closes the screens of a player in a portal
  once per screen: closing the chat repeatedly also took the HUD away, and that was the black
  `portal-overlay-1s` frame.

## Conclusion

Yes, natively. The Twilight Forest is a Bedrock custom dimension defined by F4's behavior pack;
the server and every 1.26.50+ client (with or without the client mod) know its id and height
range from the game itself. F4 adds the fixed time (hook + per-player clock for vanilla clients),
the teleporter and the portal on top.

Limits:
- The engine's generator for custom dimensions is `void`. F5 replaces the chunk generator of this
  dimension (`CustomDimension::createGenerator` / its generator creator) with the TF pipeline; until
  then the dimension is empty and portals built there float at the bottom of the world.
- Bedrock functions have no namespaces, and F4's pack paths are `**/dimension/**`: TF's
  functions are `/function twilightforest/dimension/tp_twilight` and `.../tp_overworld`
  (`/twilightforest tp twilight|overworld` do the same).
- `gf::world::teleport` to another dimension is asynchronous (the client loads the dimension): it
  returns true once the move started; `portal::onTeleported` fires on arrival, and a move that
  does not complete within 10 s is logged and told to the player.
- A non-player entity that goes through a portal arrives as a copy with a new unique id (Java keeps
  the UUID).
- Time-of-day dependent vanilla logic that reads the level time directly (not the dimension's time
  of day) still follows the overworld clock.

## Fallback and follow-ups

- If a game update drops data-driven dimensions, the MoreDimensions route (a dimension factory hook
  plus client-side id remapping) is the fallback; `gf::world` hides the backend.
- F5: install the TF generator for `world::twilight()` and keep `kMinY`/`kHeight`.
- F12/U15: the dimension's sky, fog and weather providers key on `world::twilight()`.
