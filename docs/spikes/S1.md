# S1: Can the Twilight Forest be a real dimension on Bedrock, for the client mod and for vanilla clients?

| | |
|---|---|
| Unit | F4 |
| Status | answered |
| Date | 2026-11-02 |
| Game / LeviLamina | 1.26.51 / 26.51.7 |

## Question

Can GloamingForest register `twilightforest:twilight_forest` as a dimension of its own with TF's
`twilight_forest_type` values (min_y -32, height 288, fixed time 13000, coordinate scale 0.125, beds
work), move players (SimulatedPlayers, client-mod players and vanilla Bedrock clients joining a BDS)
into it and back, and have every client render its chunks from y -32 to y 255?

## Why it matters

Every landmark, biome, mob and boss of TF lives in this dimension; every W1 selftest that needs it
calls `ctx.inTwilight()`, and the vanilla-client matrix needs vanilla clients to enter it. The
plan allowed two routes: the game's own dimension definition, or the vendored MoreDimensions route
(fake dimension ids remapped for clients).

## What was tried

1. **Native custom dimension (chosen).** Bedrock 1.26.50 ships data-driven custom dimensions:
   `SharedTypes::v1_26_50::DimensionDefinition` (`minecraft:dimension` with the components
   `minecraft:generation`, `minecraft:dimension_height` / `minecraft:dimension_bounds`,
   `minecraft:default_biome`),
   `DimensionDefinitionGroup` (read from the behavior packs), `CustomDimension`,
   `DimensionManager::_registerCustomDimensionWithFactory` and the `DimensionDataPacket` that sends
   the definitions (min y, height range, generator, id) to every client. F4 ships
   `assets/pack/bp/dimensions/dimension/twilight_forest.json` (format 1.21.0, void generator,
   `minecraft:dimension_bounds` min -32 / max 256, default biome `minecraft:forest`); the engine
   reads it from a sub-folder of `dimensions/`, registers it at world start and gives it an id
   from the world's dimension name table (1000, the first custom id; it is persisted with the
   world and stays the same across restarts). The format: `DimensionDataLoader` (a
   `Puv::SlicedLoader` of `DimensionDefinition::DimensionDocument`) picks its parser by
   `format_version`. With 1.26.50 (the v1_26_50 parser: `minecraft:dimension_height` `min_y` /
   `height_range`) the file registers, but the client's content log warns "[Dimensions][warning]
   ... format_version: this member was found in the input, but is not present in the Schema",
   since that schema has no `format_version`. W0d probes, one client run with three more files:
   1.26.60 fails ("unsupported version: no parser available for version 1.26.60"), a
   `format_version` moved into `minecraft:dimension` registers but warns about it there, and
   1.21.0 picks the older parser, whose schema holds `format_version` (no warning) and the height
   as `minecraft:dimension_bounds` `min` / `max` (with `dimension_height` it read a height range of
   0 and failed: "height_range must be greater than zero"). With 1.21.0 and
   `dimension_bounds` the dimension registers on server and client as `y -32..256` and the client's
   content log has no `[Dimensions]` line. Code: `src/feature/dimension/Dimension.cpp` (diagnostic hooks on
   `ScriptCustomDimensionRegistry::_onReadyForCustomDimensionRegistration` and
   `DimensionManager::_registerCustomDimensionWithFactory`, both MCAPI), `World.cpp`
   (`DimensionManager::tryGetDimensionType`, `Level::getOrCreateDimension`).
2. **TF's dimension_type values the definition can't hold** are added in code:
   - `fixed_time` 13000: a hook on `Dimension::$getTimeOfDay` (virtual, MCAPI) answers the time of
     day of 13000 for the Twilight Forest whatever the level time (server lighting / spawning and
     the client mod's sky).
   - Vanilla clients render the sky from their own clock, so they get a per-player clock
     (`Clock.cpp`). In 1.26 the client clock follows three packets, all of which are corrected for a
     player in the Twilight Forest:
     - `SetTimePacket` (level time),
     - `SyncWorldClocksPacket` (new in 1.26: the world clock registry, `WorldClockRegistryServer`;
       the clock named `VanillaWorldClocks::OVERWORLD_CLOCK_NAME()` is the one the sky follows; it
       is sent at join as `InitializeRegistryData` and when synced as `SyncStateData`),
     - `GameRulesChangedPacket` (doDaylightCycle).
     Packets for one player (`ServerPlayer::$sendNetworkPacket`) are corrected in place before they
     are sent and restored afterwards. Packets the `LoopbackPacketSender` sends to several players
     are followed by a corrected one in the same tick. Entering / leaving players get the Twilight
     / world clock, and the Twilight clock is sent again every 5 s. What really leaves for each
     connection (`NetworkSystem::_sendInternal`) is recorded, and the selftest
     `dimension.clock_watch` checks for 20 s that every tick of a remote player in the dimension
     ends with the time of day 13000.
   - `has_raids` false: a hook on `Village::triggerRaid` (MCAPI) refuses raids of villages in the
     dimension.
   - `ambient_light` 0.01: Bedrock keeps Java's `DimensionType#brightness` table per dimension
     (`DimensionBrightnessRamp::mBrightnessRamp`, 16 floats built from a base ambient value). The
     Twilight Forest's ramp is overwritten with TF's (light 0: 0.01) on the server at world start
     and, by the client mod, on the client's own dimension object when the player enters it (its
     light texture). Vanilla clients keep the game's ramp for custom dimensions.
   - `monster_spawn_light_level` uniform 0..7 and `monster_spawn_block_light_limit` 7: the
     contract extension `src/feature/ext/dimension/DimensionType.h` carries every value of
     `twilight_forest_type` (and of the three vanilla types) with Java's rules that read them
     (`Monster#isDarkEnoughToSpawn`, `DimensionType#brightness`, `Level#updateSkyBrightness`), for
     the natural spawner (F5), the spawners (F8) and the monsters' own spawn rules (U17); A8 folds
     it into the `world` contract.
   - `coordinate_scale` 0.125, `bed_works` (the engine already allows beds in the custom
     dimension: `mayRespawnViaBed()` is true), `respawn_anchor_works` false and `piglin_safe`
     false (on Bedrock anchors only work and piglins only stay unconverted in the Nether),
     `infiniburn` #infiniburn_overworld (netherrack and magma burn forever in every Bedrock
     dimension), `natural` true (`isNaturalDimension()` is true), `ultrawarm` / `has_ceiling` false
     and `has_skylight` true (the definition's defaults).
   - `/execute in twilightforest:twilight_forest` accepts the custom dimension's identifier
     (selftest `dimension.execute_in`), but Bedrock's `execute in` keeps the X / Z it had. Java's
     scales them (`CommandSourceStack#withLevel` multiplies the execution position by
     `DimensionType.getTeleportationScale(from, to)`), so TF's functions `tp_twilight` /
     `tp_overworld` (`execute in <dimension> run tp @p ~ ~ ~`) take overworld (100, y, 100) to
     (800, y, 800) in the Twilight Forest and back. TF's `/twilightforest` has no subcommand for
     this, and the mod's command tree stays TF's: the functions run the vanilla
     `scriptevent twilightforest:tp_twilight` / `...:tp_overworld`, which F4 handles
     (`ScriptEventCommand::$execute`, MCAPI) with Java's semantics: the execution position scaled
     into the destination, the nearest player (@p) teleported there with its rotation (selftest
     `dimension.functions`: overworld 103.5 70 111.5 -> Twilight Forest 828 70 892 and back). A
     probe showed that a behavior pack function may call a mod command on BDS as well
     (`commands.function.success`); a hidden subcommand was the alternative.
3. **Areas without players.** Java's teleporter loads the destination synchronously and holds it
   with a PORTAL ticket. Bedrock loads chunks around players and ticking areas only; a transient
   ticking area (`TickingAreasManager::addTransientScopedArea`, MCAPI; not saved with the world,
   removed with `removeScopedAreas`) loads the destination while the traveller waits at home
   (`AreaLoader.cpp`, selftest `dimension.area_loader`: an area 77000 blocks away loads without a
   player). For a player the destination starts loading while it waits in the portal, 2 s before
   the trip, so the trip leaves on its 61st tick as in Java. A ticking area removed while its
   chunks are halfway through the engine's generation pipeline strands them: a chunk whose next
   step was requested by that area (typically one at `LightingFinished`, 10) never moves on, not
   even for a fresh area asked for over it later (W0d: `dimension.teleport_riders` failed in the
   full run after a radius-0 hold over the destination column was dropped the tick before the
   trip; the TickingAreasManager then held only the three `gf_selftest` standalone areas in the
   overworld and F4's own area in the Twilight Forest, so no other unit leaked areas and no
   ticking-area limit was hit). So the loader keeps a released area until its own loading is
   over, with no time cap: after 5 minutes it goes only once no chunk of it or its ring is in the
   pipeline any more (a warning), otherwise it stays until it settles or the world closes (a
   warning). Selftest `dimension.area_handover` forces the race (an area asked for without
   waiting and released two ticks later, then the destination over it): every destination loads.
   **Limitation for W1 units:** chunks stranded by someone else (another unit's ticking area or
   chunk view removed mid-load) cannot be recovered on the server. Tried in W0d with a forced
   strand (3 areas removed at once mid-load): 30 chunks stuck at `LightingFinished` (10) were
   queued again for their next step (`LevelChunkBuilderData::enqueueChunkForProcessing` with
   their state, MCAPI) and their neighbours' blocked steps checked again
   (`ChunkSource::_checkForUnblockingChunks`, MCAPI), every 40 ticks for 700+ ticks, and a larger
   area (2 more chunks each side) was asked for over them: none of them moved on
   (`_createOrReplaceGridAreaMap`, which rebuilds a chunk's generation grid, is exported on the
   client only). So a unit must never remove a ticking area (or drop a chunk view) while its
   chunks are loading: hold it until it is loaded (`ITickingArea::isDoneLoadingScoped` / its
   view's `isDoneLoading`). A wait of the loader that meets stranded chunks (a chunk of the area
   or its ring in one waiting state for 100 ticks) logs a warning naming them, and a trip whose
   destination does not load logs it and tells the player ("The teleport to ... did not
   complete."); a shared area that does not load gets one more try with a fresh one.
   `gf::world::teleport` uses the same
   machinery: the destination loads first, then the player moves there with its riders (on
   Bedrock a player's shoulder parrots ride it; Java keeps them in the player's data, so they
   travel along). Items, mobs and vehicles travel too: the engine's own cross-dimension move of a
   non-player actor lost the item (`Actor::teleport` to another dimension: the entity was neither
   found nor marked removed afterwards), so they move as Java moves them, as a copy made in the
   destination (`Actor::save` + `BlockSource::spawnActor`) with the original removed.
4. **The portal's water.** `TFPortalBlock#getFluidState` answers level-1 flowing water (1/9 of a
   block high), so every Java rule that asks for the fluid there sees water. A Bedrock custom block
   has no partial fluid: a water layer would be a full source block the client swims in. Each
   rule is therefore reproduced on its own, on the server (`PortalFluid.cpp`, `Portal.cpp`):
   - sugar cane next to a portal keeps its water (`SugarCaneBlock::$mayPlace`, MCAPI);
   - explosions: Java takes the larger of the block's resistance (0) and the fluid's (water 100).
     Bedrock's explosion rays read each block's resistance inline from its permutation's direct
     data: a hook on `BlockType::getExplosionResistance` (MCAPI) answered the water's value but
     was not consulted (round 4: the portal broke, `the portal blocks are still there (0 of 4)`),
     and the block JSON can't hold 100 because the block itself keeps TF's 0 (F1's
     `import.behaviour` checks it; `explosion_resistance` 500 failed it with `100, TF gives 0`).
     So `Explosion::explode` (MCAPI) gives the portal's two permutations the water's resistance
     while it runs and TF's 0 again afterwards, with atomic writes. The window is shared by every
     thread (a mutex-guarded count of the explosions in flight, opened and closed by a scope):
     the first explosion raises the value, the last one to end puts back what the first found,
     so nested explosions, explosions on two threads and an explosion that throws all leave TF's
     0 behind (selftest `dimension.explosion_window`). An explosion that breaks the pool's grass
     rim leaves the portal to its neighbour rule (`TFPortalBlock#neighborChanged`, through F11's
     `gf::blockevents`), which turns it back into water as TF does. W0c: the old test read the
     block left at the pool after explosions whose random rays broke the rim in the `full` run
     (water's 100 there). `dimension.portal_explosion` now has two deterministic cases (rays start
     at radius x 0.7..1.3 and lose 0.225 per 0.3-block step plus (resistance + 0.3) x 0.3 per
     block): two power-0.75 explosions 0.25 above the middle of the pool break the glass on it and
     reach every portal block, but never the rim (the portal stays a portal); one power-1.5
     explosion 0.25 above a rim block breaks it and reaches the portal block beside it (the
     portal blocks resist, and the neighbour rule turns the whole pool into water);
   - the neighbour rule reads the floor and the four sides in Java's order; Java's getBlockState
     loads a chunk that is not loaded, so when the rule reaches a block whose chunk is not loaded
     the check waits: the chunks around the portal block are loaded for it (a transient ticking
     area) and it runs again (`dimension.portal_neighbor_unloaded`: two portal blocks on the
     border of a loaded area, one with a broken frame turns into water, the whole one stays);
   - an entity whose box reaches below the water's surface is in water
     (`Entity#updateFluidHeightAndDoFluidPushing`): its fall damage is cancelled through the
     combat contract (`LivingEntity#checkFallDamage` looks for water before the landing hurts),
     its fall distance resets, its fire goes out, it splashes when it enters (sound and splash
     particles), and water-sensitive mobs (endermen, blazes, snow golems, striders) take drowning
     damage (`LivingEntity#aiStep`);
   - farmland with a portal within 4 blocks (its level or one above) stays moist
     (`FarmBlock#isNearWater`; a hook on `FarmBlock::randomTick`, MCAPI);
   - not reproduced: water movement (the slower walk, swimming up, items floating up). On Bedrock
     that is the client's own movement physics, and the client sees no fluid in a custom block.
5. **MoreDimensions route: not needed.** It remaps ids for clients that don't know a custom
   dimension; 1.26.50+ clients know them natively, so the remapping would only add risk.

## Conclusion

Yes, natively. The Twilight Forest is a Bedrock custom dimension defined by F4's behavior pack;
the server and every 1.26.50+ client (with or without the client mod) know its id and height
range from the game itself. F4 adds the fixed time (hook + per-player clock for vanilla clients),
the teleporter and the portal on top.

Limits:
- The engine's generator for custom dimensions is `void`; F5 replaces it with the TF pipeline
  (merged): the return portals stand on the generated ground. The selftests put the terrain they
  change there back afterwards (the portal sites the teleporter builds are saved before it builds
  them).
- Bedrock functions have no namespaces, and F4's pack paths are `**/dimension/**`: TF's
  functions are `/function twilightforest/dimension/tp_twilight` and `.../tp_overworld`.
- `gf::world::teleport` to another dimension is asynchronous (the destination loads first, then the
  client loads the dimension): it returns true once the move started; `portal::onTeleported` fires
  on arrival, and a move that does not complete is logged and told to the player.
- The portal overlay for vanilla clients is a camera fade in the portal sprite's average colour
  (the client mod draws TF's own overlay through F12's HUD layers and tells the server so). A Bedrock fade has no partial alpha to hold:
  it only grows to opaque. It grows at the rate of TF's overlay (the sprite's alpha 3/4 x timer /
  60), so it is TF's 0.75 at the trip, and becomes opaque only after it, while the client shows the
  dimension change; a player who steps out while it is still translucent sees it go at once
  instead of over the timer's remaining ticks.
- A non-player entity that goes through a portal arrives as a copy with a new unique id (Java keeps
  the UUID).
- Time-of-day dependent vanilla logic that reads the level time directly (not the dimension's time
  of day) still follows the overworld clock.

## Fallback and follow-ups

- If a game update drops data-driven dimensions, the MoreDimensions route (a dimension factory hook
  plus client-side id remapping) is the fallback; `gf::world` hides the backend.
- F5: install the TF generator for `world::twilight()` and keep `kMinY`/`kHeight`.
- F12/U15: the dimension's sky, fog and weather providers key on `world::twilight()`.
- F5/F8/U17: read the monster spawn light rules and the ambient light through
  `feature/ext/dimension/DimensionType.h` (F5's `Spawn.cpp` and F8's `Boss.cpp` hard-code the same
  numbers today; A8 folds the extension into the contract and points them at it).
