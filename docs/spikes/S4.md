# S4: Can TF's entities, multipart bosses and RGB boss bars be built on Bedrock with linked actors?

| | |
|---|---|
| Unit | F8 |
| Status | answered (multipart, properties, hit test, both bar colours); converter output checked on F2's branch |
| Date | 2026-11-02 |
| Game / LeviLamina | 1.26.51 / 26.51.7 |

## Question

Can F8 give TF's 80 entity types their Java behaviour on Bedrock without engine-side entity classes:
native behaviour dispatch on BP entities, persisted per-entity state, entity properties that drive
the Molang of the resource pack, Java's goal selector, TFPart multipart bosses as linked actors
(parts that follow, take hits for the parent and are picked by the client), and TF's RGB boss bar
(exact colour with the client mod, the nearest of the 8 vanilla colours on vanilla clients)?

## Why it matters

Every mob and boss unit (U16-U17, U20-U30) builds on `gf::entity`, `gf::projectile` and
`gf::boss`. The hardest models are the multipart bosses: the Hydra (body, 3-7 heads with necks),
the Naga (12 segments), the Snow Queen (4 ice shields) and the Ur-Ghast; TF's `PartEntity`
needs three ASM patches on Java (ResolveEntitiesForRendering, ResolveEntityRenderer,
SendDirtyEntityData). The boss bar colour (TFBossBarPacket) is a visible difference on every boss.

## What was tried

1. **Behaviour dispatch** (src/feature/entity/Entity.cpp): one hook per engine event, owned by F8
   (rule R8): `Actor::tick` (behaviour tick, goals, aiStep, parts, mounts, the internal hooks of
   the projectile and boss kits; whether a type is looked at is cached per type), `Mob::$die`
   (dispatched once per life and only when the engine's death really ran: F10's death pipeline
   hooks `Mob::$die` too and may keep a mob alive), `Actor::save` / `Actor::$load` (the
   `data(actor, key)` documents travel in the actor's own save tag `GloamingForest`, so they
   follow chunk unloads, reloads and dimension changes), `Mob::$setTarget` (target filters),
   `Player::$teleportTo` (no teleporting off a hostile mount). Damage reaches `Behaviour::hurt`
   through `gf::combat` (F10 owns the hurt hook), right-clicks through `gf::interactions` (F0).
2. **Entity properties** (`setProperty`): `ActorPropertyUtils::tryQueueVariantValue` on the
   actor's `PropertyComponent` (hash of the property name), applied at once with
   `Actor::_applyPendingPropertyChanges`; read back with `PropertyContainer::getValueVariant`.
   Server-side `Actor::evalMolang("query.property(...)")` is refused by the engine ("not allowed
   in this context", content-log errors): the server evaluates Molang only in restricted contexts.
3. **Goal adapter** (Goals.cpp): Java's `GoalSelector` (insertion order, flags MOVE / LOOK / JUMP /
   TARGET, priorities, interruption, the disabled flags of `Mob#updateControlFlags`, full selector
   ticks every other tick) runs on F8's tick hook; the goals drive the engine's own
   `NavigationComponent` (`PathNavigation::moveTo / stop / canUpdatePath`, path state),
   `LookControlComponent` and the mob's motion. The engine's `GoalSelectorComponent` / `BaseGoal`
   were not subclassed: a game-side vtable is ABI-fragile and a pure C++ selector reproduces
   Java's order exactly. TF's nine shared goals and `NoClipMoveControl` are translated, with
   `DefaultRandomPos` / `RandomPos` (walk target values of Monster and Animal),
   `WalkNodeEvaluator`'s path types and maluses (`GoalUtils#hasMalus`) and
   `PathNavigation#isStableDestination` (the block data's opaque-full-block flag, checked against
   stone / glass / air at first use). Bedrock's `Path` has no callable destructor, so
   `createPath != null` is checked as Java's null cases (below the world, `!canUpdatePath()`).
4. **Linked multipart** (Multipart.cpp): every part is a real actor, `<parent id>_part` when the
   parent's unit declares one (with an integer property `<ns>:part` = the part index for its
   geometry), else F8's invisible `gloamingforest:part`. The parent moves its parts every tick
   (`Actor::moveTo` with the rotation the parent set, `ext::setPartRotation`: clients interpolate
   as for any moved actor); the size is the part's collision box (`Actor::synchronousSetSize`,
   synced by the engine). A player's attack on a part becomes the player's attack on the parent
   (`interactions::onAttack` at High priority, `Player::attack` on the parent with the part id
   readable through `ext::hurtPart()`); other damage (projectiles, explosions, magic) is forwarded
   by a `gf::combat` handler, environmental damage (suffocation, fire, falls, ...) reaches neither
   (Java's parts are not ticked as living entities; the generic part's damage sensor refuses only
   those causes). Parts are never saved (Java's are not either). Companion clients get the part
   list ("entity.multipart", read with `gf::client::entity::parts`).
5. **Custom hit test** (`entity.spike_hitbox`): Bedrock's `HitboxComponent`
   (`minecraft:custom_hit_test`: pivot + AABB per box, moved with the actor by
   `ActorSetPosSystem`) keeps extra pick boxes on one actor. A pick on such a box reaches the server
   as a hit on the actor itself, without the box: TF's `attackEntityFromPart` needs the part (the
   Hydra's heads take damage differently, the Naga's segments), so the parts stay linked actors,
   each picked by its own collision box.
6. **Boss bar** (Boss.cpp, src/client/entity): F8 sends the bar itself (no `minecraft:boss`
   component on TF bosses): `BossEventPacket` add / update_percent / update_name / update_style /
   remove per viewer (players within 160 blocks, Java's tracking), `mColor` = the nearest of
   Bedrock's 8 `BossBarColor`s to the boss's RGB (redmean distance). Companions also get
   "boss.bar" {rgb, overlay, darken, fog, progress, the viewer's title}. The first client attempt
   wrote into `ClientInstanceScreenModel::mBossInfo` and crashed: the generated header's offset of
   that member is wrong in 26.51.7 (a guarded run read a table of 1721291456096 entries). The
   client mod now hooks `ClientInstanceScreenModel::getBossBarColor(int index)` (MCAPI), the
   function the HUD reads every bar's colour through, and returns the exact RGB of the TF boss
   whose title (`getBossName(index)`; between equal titles the nearest progress,
   `getBossHealthPercentage(index)`) that bar shows; nothing of the screen model is written.
7. **Converter output on 3 models** (F2's branch `origin/f2-models` @ e4b66c3, its committed
   `assets/gen/geo`): the Hydra (`geometry.twilightforest.hydra` 25 bones / 50 cubes, plus
   `hydra_head` 3 bones `head` `jaw` `frill` and `hydra_neck` 1 bone), the Naga (`naga` and
   `naga_body` 1 bone each) and the Snow Queen (`snow_queen` 24 bones). One geometry per Java part
   model: a unit's `<id>_part` entity selects it by its `<ns>:part` index in a render controller
   (Hydra: head / neck by index), so the part convention above maps onto F2's output. The
   converted `setupAnim` of the part models (`naga_body.base`, `hydra_head.base`) are rest poses:
   Java turns these parts through the part's own yaw / pitch, which the part actors carry
   (`setPartRotation`), so the animation reads `query.body_y_rotation` / `query.target_x_rotation`.

## Conclusion

Yes:

- **Multipart as linked actors works** on the server build, the client build and vanilla clients
  (parts are ordinary actors): follow, rotation, hit routing, removal and non-persistence are
  verified. The risk is network load (one MoveActor per part per tick) and desync under lag; the
  Hydra (<= 22 actors) and Naga (13) stay well within the engine's limits. A8 runs the
  multiplayer soak.
- **Properties drive the RP's Molang**: units declare their animation state as entity properties
  (int / float / bool / enum, <= 32 per entity, `client_sync`), F8 sets them natively; the server
  cannot evaluate `query.property` itself (render controllers and animation controllers on the
  client can). Molang translation of TF's Java animations stays with F2's converter.
- **Boss bar**: the nearest of 8 colours works on every client; with the client mod the bar is
  painted in the exact RGB through `getBossBarColor`. The darken / fog flags reach companions
  only (the 26.51 `BossEventPacket` carries neither).
- **Goals**: Java's selector semantics exactly; navigation is Bedrock's pathfinder (path shapes
  differ from Java's), the candidate choice of the random positions is Java's.
- **Move controls**: a mob with an engine navigation is moved by its BP's movement component
  (Bedrock's MoveControl / FlyingMoveControl / hover / amphibious controllers); F8 steers a NoClip
  mob always (also one with a navigation) and a mob without navigation of every kind with the
  translated controllers (MoveControl, FlyingMoveControl with or without hoversInPlace,
  SmoothSwimmingMoveControl), through its motion (`entity.move_controls`).
- **Boss death animations longer than Bedrock's 20 ticks**: Bedrock removes a dead mob in its
  ECS death ticking system (`ServerMobTickDeathSystemImpl::_tick`, MCAPI, also drops the XP) after
  20 ticks. F8 hooks it and skips the engine's tick for a dying TF boss until the boss's own
  `isDeathAnimationFinished` (`gf::boss::ext::Lifecycle::deathAnimationFinished`, default
  `BossSpec::deathTicks`); then it runs the engine's tick from 19, which removes the body with
  its XP and death particles. The dead body (and its parts) stay visible on every client meanwhile
  (`entity.boss_lifecycle_hooks`: the body still there after 40 ticks, removed at 60;
  boss-dying-<mode>.png 2 s after the death). The client mod gets the death time at the death
  (`boss.death_time` 0) and counts on (Bedrock clients stop at their 20).
- **Lifecycle overrides**: every BaseTFBoss step TF's bosses override (Lich clones without loot or
  spawner, the Knight Phantoms' shared loot and last-knight chest, the Naga's and Lich's random
  chest, Hydra's own tickDeath, Lich#placeSpawner / #postRemoval) is a `Lifecycle` member, so the
  boss units never hook `die` (rule R8).
- **Late trackers**: every value `gf::entity::sync` sent (and the part list) goes again to a
  companion that starts seeing the actor (within 160 blocks, checked every 10 ticks) or joins
  (Java's `startSeenByPlayer`); `entity.sync_late` covers both.
- **Bar overlay**: Bedrock's HUD draws no notches; the client mod draws `BossBarOverlay`'s
  dividers (NOTCHED_6 / 10 / 12 / 20) over the HUD's bar (found in the HUD's visual tree,
  `boss_health_grid` / `progress_bar_for_collections`) after the HUD rendered.

## Fallback and follow-ups

- If `Actor::tick` stops being called for some actor class after an update, the dispatch moves to
  the level tick over `Level::getRuntimeActorList()` (same per-actor code).
- If `getBossBarColor` stops being the HUD's colour source after an update, ClientTFBossBar is drawn
  through F12's `hud::addLayer` (the white bar textures tinted with the RGB); vanilla clients are
  unaffected either way.
- A8: one die hook (rule R8): F8's dispatch moves into F10's post-death step.
- Deviations recorded in the PR: parts of a parent without its own part type use
  `gloamingforest:part`; units may declare `<id>_part` types (owners.tsv rows for A8).
